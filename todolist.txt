RULED: CHAPTERED MODE IS THE DELIVERY SHAPE (mentor, 2026-09-28)
=============================================================================
3-5 min per chapter x 5 chapters = 15-25 min, against a design built around a
4-minute video. An install session with a prerequisites card, a checkpoint per step
and a failure scene does NOT fit that budget -- not marginally, and not by tuning.
Five minutes is roughly four spoken commands plus a checkpoint and a pause; the uv
storyboard's FIRST chapter alone is four steps with three checkpoints.

  delivery unit      one video ~4 min   ->  a CHAPTER, 3-5 min
  total length       ~4 min              ->  15-25 min across 5 chapters
  scene budget       per video           ->  PER CHAPTER
  TARGET_MINUTES     per video           ->  per chapter (~4); the sum is the total
  source coverage    per video           ->  per chapter

A CHAPTER IS STANDALONE and must work alone: it states its start_state at the top,
and a learner arriving from a search result has not seen chapters 1-3. start_state
is the declaration that closes that gap, and _unclaimed_source_sections has no
opinion about it.

THREE CONSEQUENCES THAT ARE EASY TO MISS:
  1. Coverage is now per chapter, so the 12.3 ruling (every numbered concept claimed
     or the build refuses) applies PER CHAPTER. A 5-scene chapter cannot claim 7
     concepts, so the document's concept list is PARTITIONED across chapters and
     the partition is recorded in each chapter's start_state.
  2. AC#30's instruments become per chapter: loudness, caption geometry and the
     word-boundary check each run on that chapter's own artefact. A gate reading a
     concatenation is a control on the wrong side of a boundary, in exactly the way
     A18 was (see 12.2).
  3. The cheat-sheet card and the chapter marker are load-bearing, not polish. A
     learner joining at chapter 4 has no list of chapters 1-3's commands.

MIN_SCENES STAYS AT 5, deliberately. A 3-scene chapter is a prerequisites card, one
command and a checkpoint; the honest minimum for "a learner can tell whether it
worked" is five distinct beats. Lowering it to make a chapter fit would be tuning
the gate to the artefact -- the same error as widening a tolerance to absorb a mux.

HONEST LIMITATION: this changes the shape of the deliverable and the meaning of two
constants. It does NOT re-measure the gate against a 25-minute plan, because no
render exists at the new size. The first chapter render is the measurement, and if
the scene budget or the duration estimator is wrong at this scale, that render
will say so.

F1 -- RULED AND FIXED. LLD 12.6. No longer blocks anything.
=============================================================================
The conflict was real and is recorded, because the fix depends on understanding it:
naming a command three times, as instrument 4 requires, produced three repeated
3-grams. Satisfying the gate instead produced "try that same check once more",
which a learner listening rather than looking cannot act on. The gate was right
that the prose repeated and wrong about what to do about it.

THE FIX: spoken_command_trigrams() in storyboard.py. A flag is spelled the way TTS
says it -- `--version` becomes `dash dash version` -- so the protected span matches
the NARRATION being gated, not the command text. A string search would protect
"uv --version" while the text under examination is "uv, dash, dash, version", and
the two never match. That is why it is a tokeniser.

It is also NARROW, which is the part that matters: only trigrams inside a command's
spoken span are protected. Measured, the gate went 3 findings -> 1, and the survivor
was "dash version and" -- genuine prose repetition, because two sentences both said
"and" after the command. Varying the connective cleared it. THE GATE KEPT CATCHING
PROSE REPETITION, which is the behaviour it exists for.

ONCE, AND IT IS WORTH READING
Original note, kept because the fix is unintuitive without the conflict:
This blocks any real video and is a small design decision, not a code problem.

Instrument 4 requires the narration to NAME the command on screen. The trigram gate
forbids a repeated 3-word phrase. Naming a command three times produces three
repeated 3-grams -- measured: "run uv dash" x3, "uv dash dash" x3, "dash dash
version" x3.

Removing the repetition to satisfy the gate produced "try that same check once
more", which a learner LISTENING rather than looking cannot act on. The gate was
right that the prose repeated, and wrong about what to do about it.

THE RULING NEEDED: the trigram ban applies to PROSE, not to a command the
storyboard declares on screen. The gate must skip windows that fall inside a
declared command string.

Until then, the spike fixture carries a V1_TRIGRAM_EXEMPTION block and run_v1.py
PRINTS IT AND RENDERS ANYWAY. Scoped to the spike, not a precedent. A silent
bypass would be the defect this project keeps recording.
